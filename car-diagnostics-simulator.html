A free car diagnostics simulator for learning engine fault-finding

Practise diagnosing a petrol engine management system the way a workshop does it: read the customer's complaint, check the symptoms, use a multimeter, oscilloscope, scan tool and fuel pressure gauge, then fix the fault with as few new parts as possible. It is built for automotive apprentices, TAFE students and anyone curious about how an engine is controlled.

Practice mode has no fault, so you can learn what good readings look like. Challenge mode picks one of ten faults at random, from a failed crank sensor to a corroded injector wire that only shows up on the scope.

How to use it

  1. Read the work order, then turn the ignition on and try to start the car.
  2. Read the fault codes with the scan tool, and check live data.
  3. Click a part to see its pins. Choose the red or black probe, then click a pin to measure. Use the wiring diagram to trace circuits.
  4. To measure resistance, unplug the part first. To check a wire, unplug both ends.
  5. Replace the faulty part or repair the wire, clear the codes, turn the key off and hand the car back.

Good readings to compare against

  • Battery: 12.4 to 12.6 V key on, 10 V or more cranking, about 14 V running.
  • Injector: about 13 Ω. Ignition coil primary: about 0.6 Ω. Crank sensor: about 820 Ω.
  • Coolant sensor signal: about 2.5 V cold, under 0.5 V warm.
  • MAP signal: about 4 V key on, about 1.6 V at idle. Fuel pressure: about 3 bar.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the Electude simulator?

No. It is an independent, simplified simulator inspired by workshop training tools like it. The car, circuits and faults are made up for learning, so readings are realistic but not taken from a specific vehicle.

What faults can it have?

Challenge mode picks one of ten: a failed crankshaft sensor, an open coolant sensor wire, a dead injector, a failed ignition coil, a bad fuel pump relay, an open MAP sensor reference wire, a worn spark plug, a worn-out battery, a corroded injector supply wire and a blown EFI fuse.

Why can I not measure resistance?

Resistance is measured with the circuit unpowered. Unplug the part to measure the part itself. To measure a wire, unplug the part at one end and the ECM at the other.

How is the job graded?

You get an A for fixing it in about 12 minutes or less without replacing any part that was not faulty. Replacing good parts and taking longer lowers the grade, just as it would cost a real customer more.
