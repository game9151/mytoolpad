WAM calculator for Australian universities

Your weighted average mark (WAM) is the average of your unit marks, weighted by credit points. A 12-point unit counts twice as much as a 6-point unit. Many universities use it for honours entry, scholarships and graduate programs.

WAM = sum of (mark × credit points) ÷ total credit points. Marks of 72, 80 and 65 in three 6-point units give (432 + 480 + 390) ÷ 18 = 72.33.

How to use it

  1. Enter the mark out of 100 and the credit points for each unit.
  2. Turn on the weighting column if your faculty weights later years more heavily.
  3. Set a target to see the average you need on the units you have left.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between WAM and GPA?

WAM averages your actual marks out of 100. GPA converts each mark into grade points first, such as 7 for a High Distinction, so two students with the same GPA can have different WAMs.

Do fails count in my WAM?

At most universities, yes: a failed unit counts at the mark you received. Some exclude units you later repeated. Check your university handbook.

What is a good WAM?

It depends on the university and course. As a rough guide, 75 or above is in the Distinction range and is often the bar for honours and many scholarships.

Is my data saved?

Your units are saved in this browser only, so they are still here next time. Nothing is sent anywhere.
